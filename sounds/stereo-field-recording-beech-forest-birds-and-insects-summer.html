Stereo Field Recording, Beech Forest • Birds and Insects, Summer

Stereo field recording from a beech forest in Western Poland — a summer afternoon in old-growth woodland. Layered bird calls, woodpecker in the distance, light insect activity. No wind on mic, no traffic.

A massive beech had come down, root plate and all — the crater it left is deep enough to stand in. We placed the mics on either side of the trunk, out of frame, and walked back to an old log maybe thirty steps away to stay quiet. The woodpecker started almost immediately. A full chorus came in from the canopy. The copper-colored leaf floor and the moss on the bark were the only things moving.

Sound is layered but calm: birds at multiple distances, insects underneath, leaves occasionally brushing. No sudden sounds.

Gear: NT1 microphone pair + Zoom F3 — stereo pair left and right of the fallen trunk, hidden behind an adjacent tree.

For most people, a fallen tree is just an obstacle on the trail. We're guests in this place, and we stayed long enough to watch it become something else. Recorded together with my son, who is on the autism spectrum. No filters, no noise reduction, no AI.
