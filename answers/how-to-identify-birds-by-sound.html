How to identify birds by sound?

To identify a bird by sound, listen for rhythm, pitch, repeated phrases and the place you are in, then confirm it with a recording or an app such as Merlin Bird ID or BirdNET.

Bird Sounds That Don't Stop | Flooded Forest Backwater in Summer

Start with the pattern. Is it one note repeated (like a cuckoo), a fast trill, a flute-like melody (blackbird, golden oriole) or a drumming (woodpecker)? Then note the pitch and whether phrases repeat. Habitat and season narrow it down: reed bed, beech forest, meadow or garden. Time of day matters too, because the dawn chorus has a typical order.

Learn one species at a time. Begin with the common ones in Europe (blackbird, robin, great tit), then add others. Recordings with marked species are good training. In our flooded forest backwater recording the description marks where the blackbird, great spotted woodpecker, raven and green woodpecker are heard; the garden and lake recordings mark golden oriole, yellowhammer, robin, blue tit and others.

Apps suggest a species from a few seconds of audio. Treat the answer as a hint and check it against what you hear. Three of our recordings with marked species are below.

Quick questions

What is the easiest bird to learn by sound?

The blackbird: a rich, flute-like song from a treetop or rooftop at dusk and dawn. Learn it first and everything else is easier to separate.

Does time of day help?

Yes. Many birds sing most at dawn, and a few, like the nightingale, sing at night.
